# Ocarina of Time Series — Planned Books

A slate of Latin picture books adapted from canonical OoT story beats. Each book maps one clearly bounded arc to one primary virtue, evaluated against the 5-question framework from the main CLAUDE.md. One arc, one want, one obstacle, one emotional through-line — the same discipline used for Fire Dragon (book 1, *Lincus et Volvāgia*, fortitūdō-5).

Ordering below is **chronological by the game's story**. A suggested **production order** is given at the end — it prioritizes simpler arcs first and spreads out fortitūdō-heavy books so the virtue ratings stay varied.

---

## Chronological Sequence

### 1. *Lincus et Arbor Magna* — "Link and the Great Tree"
**Arc:** Great Deku Tree / Gohma (Kokiri Forest opening)
**Primary virtue:** pietās (duty to the aged/wise father-figure)
**Secondary:** fortitūdō (first venture into danger)
**Want / obstacle:** The Great Deku Tree calls Link to help. Inside the tree is a monstrous eye-spider, Gohma, poisoning him. Link is the smallest Kokiri — can he help?
**Refrain candidate:** *Arbor vocat.* ("The tree calls.") — repeated as Link enters deeper.
**Through-line:** The smallest child answers the biggest call.
**Visual spine:** Glowing forest → hollow inside the tree → giant-eye spider → reverent farewell.
**Age note:** The Deku Tree dies at the end. Handle like *Bambi* / *Charlotte's Web* — lean into the dignity, not the grief. For 3.5yo, end on "he gave Link a gift" rather than dwelling.
**Why it works:** Tree-with-a-face + eye-monster inside = pure picture-book.

### 2. *Lincus et Zelda Fugit* — "Link and the Fleeing Princess"
**Arc:** Hyrule Castle courtyard + Zelda's escape
**Primary virtue:** prūdentia (Zelda *sees* the evil others miss) + fidēs (trust sealed by a gift)
**Want / obstacle:** Link carries a message to the princess; guards stand in the way; when he finds her she is spying through a window at a dark king. That night, a storm. She rides past, throws him the Ocarina.
**Refrain candidate:** *Tacē!* ("Hush!") during the sneak pages.
**Through-line:** A small boy sees what a princess sees.
**Visual spine:** Sneaking past guards (vine + window + crawl) → the courtyard → the castle on a rainy night → a white horse vanishing into the dark.
**Why it works:** Sneaking + secret letter + a princess on horseback — inherently exciting, no violence needed.

### 3. *Lincus et Rēx Dodongō* — "Link and King Dodongo"
**Arc:** Dodongo's Cavern (child Link saves the Gorons)
**Primary virtue:** cāritās (feeding the hungry) + fortitūdō
**Secondary:** this establishes the brother-bond with Darunia that pays off in book 1 (*Lincus et Volvāgia*).
**Want / obstacle:** The Gorons are starving — dragon-lizards block their food cave. Darunia has given up. Link, smallest of all, goes in alone.
**Refrain candidate:** *Gorōnēs ēsuriunt.* ("The Gorons hunger.") → transforms to *Gorōnēs iam ēdunt.* ("Now the Gorons eat.")
**Visual spine:** Hungry Gorons → cavern with walking lizards → giant boss rolls like a boulder → Link feeds it a bomb, it falls in lava → Gorons feasting → Darunia: *"frāter meus."*
**Why it works:** Explicitly prequel to *Lincus et Volvāgia* — the SAME "frāter meus" line reappears in book 1. Read them together, the refrain rings twice.

### 4. *Lincus et Piscis Magnus* — "Link and the Great Fish"
**Arc:** Jabu-Jabu's Belly (rescue Princess Ruto)
**Primary virtue:** cāritās (rescue at cost) + humilitās (carrying a princess who complains)
**Want / obstacle:** The Zora princess has been swallowed by the giant guardian fish. Link is swallowed next. Inside, he must carry her while electric jellyfish float past.
**Refrain candidate:** *Intus piscis sumus.* ("We are inside the fish.")
**Through-line:** Jonah-in-the-whale for a toddler.
**Visual spine:** Underwater city → great fish mouth → glowing pink belly → jellyfish → the boss (Barinade) → spat out onto the surface.
**Why it works:** Being INSIDE a fish is all-time picture-book material.

### 5. *Lincus et Equa Fidēlis* — "Link and the Loyal Horse"
**Arc:** Lon Lon Ranch / Epona / Malon / Ingo
**Primary virtue:** grātitūdō (memory returning a gift) + iustitia (restoring what's stolen)
**Secondary:** a quieter, pastoral book — deliberate contrast to the temple arcs.
**Want / obstacle:** Child Link meets Malon. She teaches him a song her foal loves. Seven years later the ranch has been stolen by a villain. Adult Link plays the same song. The grown horse remembers him. They race the villain for her freedom.
**Refrain candidate:** the melody itself — a wordless visual refrain (musical notation on the page). Or *Epona meminit.* ("Epona remembers.")
**Through-line:** A song planted in childhood grows back to rescue both horse and Link.
**Why it works:** Probably the emotional sweet-spot of the series for this age. Horses + music + time-jump payoff.

### 6. *Lincus et Gladius Tempōris* — "Link and the Sword of Time"
**Arc:** Temple of Time — three stones placed, Master Sword drawn, seven-year sleep
**Primary virtue:** magnanimitās (accepting a great calling) + spēs
**Want / obstacle:** Three sacred stones. A door that will not open. An ocarina. The door opens. A sword waits. Link takes it — and vanishes.
**Refrain candidate:** *Ūnum. Duo. Tria.* (counting the stones) — or the Song of Time melody.
**Through-line:** A child puts down what's in his hands to take up something bigger.
**Visual spine:** Sacred stones glowing → Door of Time → pedestal → shaft of light → Link disappears into radiance → adult Link waking.
**Why it works:** A meditative, short book — fewer pages, more atmosphere. Bridges child-era and adult-era books.

### 7. *Lincus et Silva Sacra* — "Link and the Sacred Forest"
**Arc:** Forest Temple / Phantom Ganon / Saria (Sage of Forest)
**Primary virtue:** amīcitia / fidēs (a friendship that survives a seven-year separation)
**Want / obstacle:** Adult Link returns to the forest. Saria, his childhood friend, is gone — trapped in a temple haunted by a dark rider. Link must find her again.
**Refrain candidate:** *Sāria exspectat.* ("Saria waits.")
**Through-line:** The friend you loved as a child is still waiting for you.
**Visual spine:** Overgrown forest → temple stairs → ghost-rider (Phantom Ganon) emerging from paintings → defeated → Saria on a tree-stump playing the ocarina.
**Age note:** Phantom Ganon is the least scary temple boss — a ghostly knight on a pale horse. Leans magical, not gory.

### 8. *Lincus et Volvāgia* — ✅ **BOOK 1 — DONE**
Fortitūdō-5 / cāritās-3.

### 9. *Lincus et Aqua Profunda* — "Link and the Deep Water"
**Arc:** Water Temple / Morpha
**Primary virtue:** patientia / perseverantia (the Water Temple endures; the water rises and falls; Link keeps going)
**Want / obstacle:** Lake Hylia has drained. At the bottom, a flooded temple. Link dives, and dives again, and again — the water changes on every level. At the heart, a creature made of water itself.
**Refrain candidate:** *Aqua crēscit. Aqua dēcrēscit.* ("The water rises. The water falls.")
**Through-line:** Patience in the long, cold dark.
**Visual spine:** Dry lake → sinking → water-level puzzles shown as repeated rising/falling imagery → tentacle-monster with a glowing core → Ruto (now Sage of Water).
**Why it works:** Patientia is rare in the collection. Water Temple's level-changing mechanic translates beautifully as a visual refrain.

### 10. *Lincus et Rēgīna Deserti* — "Link and the Queen of the Desert"
**Arc:** Spirit Temple / Twinrova / Nabooru (Sage of Spirit)
**Primary virtue:** prūdentia (seeing which attack to reflect, when, at whom)
**Want / obstacle:** Across the sand sea stands a colossus. The bandit queen Nabooru is trapped inside. Two witches hurl ice and fire. Link carries a mirror shield.
**Refrain candidate:** *Ignem ad ignem. Glaciem ad glaciem.* ("Fire to fire. Ice to ice.")
**Through-line:** A small boy turns the enemy's weapon against them by seeing correctly.
**Visual spine:** Desert dunes → stone colossus face → interior with twin witch-sisters → mirror reflections → the witches aging to dust → Nabooru freed.
**Age note:** The Twinrova are "sexy witches" in-game — redesign as wizened crones. The mirror-reflection mechanic is the star.

### 11. *Lincus et Ganondorfus* — "Link and Ganondorf" *(SERIES FINALE)*
**Arc:** Ganon's Castle / final confrontation
**Primary virtue:** fortitūdō + fidēs (trusting Zelda's light at the last moment)
**Want / obstacle:** A dark tower rises from the lake. Link climbs it. At the top, an organ. A king. A sword-fight across a crumbling hall.
**Refrain candidate:** the bracket of the whole series — *Nōn fugit Lincus* re-invoked one final time, tying back to book 1.
**Through-line:** Everything the hero has carried comes down to one last stand.
**Visual spine:** Dark tower rising → pipe organ in shadow → sword duel → castle collapsing → Zelda binding Ganon with light.
**Age note:** Ganon's second "beast form" is too horror-adjacent — end the book at Ganondorf's defeat in the tower, before the transformation. The game's own cutscene between those forms makes a natural book-ending.

## Deliberately Deferred

- **Shadow Temple / Bongo Bongo.** The game's "House of the Dead": bones, torture implements, disembodied drumming hands. Genuinely too horror-grim for a 3.5-year-old. Revisit when the child is 6+. When we do adapt it, the virtue angle would be **fortitūdō-sustinere** (facing the dark alone) + **pietās** (honoring the unquiet dead — Impa's family role as Sheikah shadow-keepers).
- **Sheik's songs** (wandering musical encounters). Individually too thin to carry a book; could work as a single bundled volume later — "Lincus et Septem Cantūs" ("Link and the Seven Songs") — but not yet.
- **Ice Cavern**, **Gerudo fortress stealth**, **Running Man**, etc. — too minor; fold into adjacent books if needed.

## Virtue Coverage Check

With book 1 done and 10 planned, the virtue spread across the OoT series alone is:

| Virtue | Book(s) | Rating(s) |
|---|---|---|
| fortitūdō | 1 (Volvāgia), 11 (Ganondorf) | 5, 4 |
| cāritās | 3 (Dodongo), 4 (Jabu-Jabu) | 4, 4 |
| pietās | 1 (Deku Tree) | 4 |
| prūdentia | 2 (Zelda Flees), 10 (Desert Queen) | 3, 4 |
| fidēs | 2 (Zelda Flees), 7 (Saria), 11 (Ganondorf) | 3, 4, 3 |
| patientia / perseverantia | 9 (Water Temple) | 4 |
| magnanimitās / spēs | 6 (Sword of Time) | 3 |
| grātitūdō / iustitia | 5 (Epona) | 3 |
| humilitās | 4 (Jabu-Jabu secondary) | 2 |

**Gaps:** temperantia isn't served by any OoT arc — OoT's a courage-and-justice game, not a self-governance game. That's fine; pick up temperantia from other series.

**Over-representation:** fortitūdō shows up as secondary in many of these even when it's not primary (which is fine — Aquinas: "courage is every virtue at the testing point"). Just don't let it dominate virtue ratings.

## Suggested Production Order

Not chronological — prioritizes craft lessons, virtue spread, and complexity ramp:

1. ✅ *Lincus et Volvāgia* (done)
2. *Lincus et Rēx Dodongō* — sibling book to #1 (both Goron-arcs, shared refrain "frāter meus"). Reuse Goron City refs, Darunia ref. Cheap and fast.
3. *Lincus et Equa Fidēlis* (Epona) — tonal contrast after two action books. Pastoral, quiet, musical. New locations (Lon Lon Ranch) but few characters.
4. *Lincus et Arbor Magna* (Great Deku Tree) — introduces child Link + Kokiri Forest as a new visual series spine. Pietās beat.
5. *Lincus et Piscis Magnus* (Jabu-Jabu) — cāritās + humility; adventure inside a fish.
6. *Lincus et Zelda Fugit* — sneaking + castle + Zelda. Establishes Zelda + Impa visuals for the series.
7. *Lincus et Gladius Tempōris* — the bridge book; meditative; fewer pages.
8. *Lincus et Silva Sacra* (Forest Temple) — adult Link returning. Phantom Ganon.
9. *Lincus et Aqua Profunda* (Water Temple) — the hardest to do well. Save for when we've mastered consistency.
10. *Lincus et Rēgīna Deserti* (Spirit Temple) — prūdentia book.
11. *Lincus et Ganondorfus* — the finale. Do last; it should feel earned.

## Series Production Notes

- **Child Link vs. adult Link** are visually distinct and need different character refs. Books 1, 2, 3, 4, first half of 5, 6 are **child Link** (Toon-Link-ish chibi proportions but in OoT style, green tunic). Books 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, second half of 5 are **adult Link**. Plan the refs accordingly.
- **Recurring characters** that need stable refs across the series: child Link, adult Link, Zelda (child and adult/Sheik), Darunia, Ruto (child and adult), Saria, Malon (child and adult), Impa, Navi (the fairy), Ganondorf, Epona. Build a character sheet library in `reference_images/zelda_oot/characters/` as we go — each new book should add refs we don't have yet.
- **Recurring locations** to lock in early: Kokiri Forest (books 1, 7), Hyrule Castle (books 2, 11), Lon Lon Ranch (book 5), Kakariko Village (minor, several books), Temple of Time (book 6).
- **The Master Sword** (Gladius Erīlis) is visible from book 6 onward — include a canonical visual.
- **Navi the fairy** is Link's companion the whole game. Decide up front whether she's a named on-page character across the series or stays in the background. Recommend: include her as a quiet visual presence (floating light), only name her in one or two books.
- **Macrons on Latin names**: Volvāgia, Darūnia, Cōcīrus, Zōra, Gerudō, Sāria, Rutō, Sēch, Cacarīcum — set in `reference_images/zelda_oot/CLAUDE.md` already; add new names there as they appear.
